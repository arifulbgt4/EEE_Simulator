# ADR-0001: Hierarchical Multi-Fidelity Product Vision

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-07-11
- Decision owners: Project maintainers
- Related requirements: `REQ-001`, `REQ-002`, `REQ-003`, `REQ-025`, `REQ-027`, `REQ-028`, `REQ-036`, `REQ-037`, `REQ-038`

## Context

One numerical method cannot feasibly provide full device physics, transistor switching, gate timing, RTL behavior, CPU architecture, and complete-system execution at useful interactive scale. Detailed models are valuable for selected devices and small circuits, while larger systems require switch, event, RTL, cycle, architecture, or ISA abstractions. [Source PDF, pp. 3-4, 12-21, 42-44]

The product still needs one understandable learning path from a component to a complete educational computer and a way to inspect a selected subsystem in more detail. [Source PDF, pp. 1-2, 13-17]

## Decision

The platform SHALL be one hierarchical, multi-fidelity product rather than a single universal solver.

- A project may contain electrical, thermal, digital, RTL, architecture, and functional blocks at different declared fidelity levels.
- Every block boundary shall declare ports, domains, units, timing semantics, and supported state exchange.
- A user may substitute a compatible implementation at a different fidelity without rewiring the surrounding design.
- The UI shall always identify the active model and fidelity; it shall not imply that an abstract result is transistor-accurate.
- Detailed component and transistor models are used where their scale is useful. Gate, RTL, cycle, architecture, and ISA models are required at larger scales.
- Work advances through release gates: realistic electronics first, then digital logic and memory, educational CPUs, a small complete computer, and finally architecture, GPU, and research systems. [Source PDF, pp. 32-41, 43-44]

Physical appearance, schematic symbols, packages, and executable behavior remain separate contracts. A realistic rendering does not raise a model's fidelity or accuracy envelope.

## Consequences

- Co-simulation adapters and deterministic boundary semantics are first-class platform contracts.
- Model authors must publish supported analyses, fidelity, limits, provenance, and validation evidence.
- Large-system features cannot bypass earlier release gates merely because an external engine is available.
- Results from different fidelity levels may be compared only within their declared interfaces and accuracy envelopes.

## Rejected alternatives

- **One SPICE-class model for the entire system:** infeasible for modern CPU, GPU, and large-memory scale. [Source PDF, pp. 12-13, 18-21]
- **A collection of unrelated simulators:** fails the unified hierarchy, substitution, and learning-path goals.
- **Functional-only simulation:** cannot deliver the realistic, non-ideal electronics foundation.

## Compliance

A change that removes hierarchy, hides active fidelity, or promises full-transistor modern-system simulation requires a superseding ADR.
