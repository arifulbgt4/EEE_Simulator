# ADR-0007: Rendering, Physical Representation, and WebGPU Policy

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-07-11
- Decision owners: Project maintainers
- Related requirements: `REQ-003`, `REQ-004`, `REQ-007`, `REQ-033`, `REQ-034`, `REQ-037`, `REQ-038`

## Context

The editor must support component placement, wiring, waveforms, heat maps, voltage/current visualization, hierarchy, and simulation controls. The source brief identifies Canvas, WebGL or WebGPU, SVG, and off-main-thread rendering tools, while warning that GPU suitability depends on the workload. [Source PDF, pp. 22-25, 30-31, 39]

Users also need basic components to resemble real hardware and integrated circuits to support distinct, reusable package designs without sacrificing electrical clarity.

## Decision

- Canvas 2D shall be the default interactive schematic and physical-component renderer.
- WebGL2 shall be the scale path for large scenes, dense overlays, and accelerated visualization.
- SVG shall be an export format, not the required interactive scene implementation.
- WebGPU may be enabled only for a bounded workload that passes reproducible correctness, performance, memory, power, compatibility, and fallback benchmarks. [Source PDF, pp. 24-25]
- Viewport culling, level of detail, selective probing, bounded waveform retention, and Worker or OffscreenCanvas processing shall protect responsiveness where supported. [Source PDF, pp. 23, 29-31, 39]
- Every released basic component shall have an electrically unambiguous schematic symbol and a linked, original, scalable physical representation. Switching views preserves identity, connectivity, parameter values, model binding, and simulation state.
- Physical views shall expose applicable body shape, leads or terminals, polarity, pin-one orientation, value or color coding, and safety markings. Zoom simplification and accessible non-color cues are mandatory.
- IC device function, schematic symbol, electrical model, physical package, and optional footprint metadata shall remain separate definitions. A parametric package definition controls body, pins or pads, numbering, pitch, orientation marks, labels, colors, and applicable thermal pads.
- Rendering realism shall never be presented as dimensional, manufacturing, or simulation accuracy. Current-particle animation indicates direction and relative magnitude, not literal electron speed. [Source PDF, p. 31]

## Consequences

- Schematic and physical views share selection, wiring, state, and diagnostics but may use different visual assets.
- Package reuse and per-device customization require explicit, validated pin mapping.
- Every accelerated path needs a behaviorally equivalent fallback.
- Original assets and provenance records avoid copying protected symbol or package artwork.

## Rejected alternatives

- **DOM/SVG for every interactive object:** does not meet the selected large-scene performance path.
- **WebGPU by default:** browser availability and irregular sparse workloads do not justify it without evidence. [Source PDF, pp. 24-25]
- **Photorealistic bodies instead of schematic symbols:** reduce electrical clarity and confuse rendering with model accuracy.
- **Embedding package geometry in every IC model:** prevents reuse, safe pin-map validation, and independent evolution.

## Compliance

Changing the default renderer, WebGPU gate, dual-view identity guarantee, or device/package separation requires a superseding ADR.
