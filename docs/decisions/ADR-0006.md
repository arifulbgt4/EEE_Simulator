# ADR-0006: Deterministic Timestamped Co-Simulation Scheduler

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-07-11
- Decision owners: Project maintainers
- Related requirements: `REQ-010`, `REQ-013`, `REQ-018`, `REQ-019`, `REQ-020`, `REQ-021`

## Context

Analog solvers advance by timesteps while digital logic, clocks, RTL blocks, thermal models, and external engines may advance by discrete events or separate steps. A global coordinator is required to exchange boundary state in a defined order. [Source PDF, pp. 27-28]

Uncontrolled ordering between engines can create inconsistent results, one of the source study's central integration risks. [Source PDF, p. 40]

## Decision

- One global scheduler shall coordinate analog timesteps, digital events, clocks, thermal updates, RTL or external-engine boundaries, waveform sampling, and checkpoints.
- Canonical simulation time shall use a versioned integer timebase. Floating-point wall-clock values shall not define event order.
- Every cross-domain boundary change shall be timestamped and ordered by a documented deterministic tie-break policy.
- An analog engine shall advance no farther than the next relevant boundary event unless an adapter explicitly supports reversible interpolation or rollback.
- Digital semantics shall include `0`, `1`, `X`, and `Z`, declared drive strength, propagation delay, contention, and timing violations. [Source PDF, pp. 14-15, 28-29, 34]
- Analog-to-digital adapters shall declare thresholds, hysteresis, and unknown regions. Digital-to-analog adapters shall declare output resistance, rise/fall time, current limit, and supply dependence. [Source PDF, p. 28]
- The scheduler shall record configuration, seed, model versions, engine versions, and ordered diagnostics needed to reproduce a run.
- Pause, checkpoint, resume, and cancellation are capabilities negotiated per engine; unsupported capabilities shall be reported before execution.

## Consequences

- Same project revision, configuration, seed, and engine versions must reproduce the same declared logical ordering.
- Parallel execution may optimize independent work but cannot change observable order.
- Adapter tests must cover simultaneous events, threshold crossings, rollback or step rejection, cancellation, and checkpoint restoration.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Independent engine clocks:** cannot guarantee stable cross-domain results.
- **Floating-point timestamps as the sole ordering key:** permit platform-dependent ties and drift.
- **Ideal voltage-only digital boundaries:** hide loading and signal-integrity behavior.

## Compliance

Changing canonical time or observable event ordering requires a superseding ADR and compatibility analysis for checkpoints and results.
